From principle to viva.

Every practical follows the order of a real laboratory session.

  1. Read the experiment

    Aim, principle, apparatus and procedure, as in the practical manual.

  2. Run the bench

    Set up the apparatus, give the drugs and watch the response develop.

  3. Record and calculate

    Fill in the observation sheet, read the graphs and work out the result.

  4. Answer for it

    Take the viva, submit to faculty and read your feedback report.

Questions colleges ask first.

Something else? Write to us or read the documentation.

Can simulations take the place of animal experiments in our practicals?

XPRISM follows the 3Rs and the CCSEA and Pharmacy Council of India framework, which allows computer-assisted modules in place of animal experiments in teaching. The simulations are teaching aids: they do not replace the approvals needed for real research involving animals. Read the ethics page.

What does the free trial include?

You can open each experiment once. A visit ends when you leave the module or after {{ TRIAL_HOURS }} hours, and reloading the page during a visit is fine. The trial has no assignments or marks. A subscription, or one free month for your college, removes the limit.

How do students join a class?

Faculty create a batch and either upload the class list as CSV or Excel, or share the batch's class code so students sign up themselves. Students added from a list get a temporary password and choose their own at first sign-in.

How are marks calculated?

The automatic score is the viva (40%), the unknown or numeric answer (30%, in experiments that have one) and a completed observation sheet (30%). It becomes provisional marks out of the assignment's maximum. Faculty review each submission and can change the marks and add feedback.

What feedback do students get after they submit?

A feedback report straight away: what each part of the practical earned, which viva questions to revise, and how many attempts are left. In exam mode the correct answers stay hidden. Students are then asked to rate the experiment, and faculty see those ratings without names.

What do we need to run it?

A current web browser. The simulations run in the student's browser, so there is nothing to install on lab computers.

What happens to student data?

Every user accepts the policies before using the lab. Students can download everything held about them and ask for correction or deletion, and each college sees only its own students. Read the privacy policy.
