Using XPRISM

How to register, run a simulation, set and grade practicals, and manage XPRISM for a college.

Getting started

Choose how to register

  • Free trial: sign up with your name, e-mail and institution, and explore each experiment once.
  • Class code: if your faculty gave you a code, use it to join their batch. You get your assignments and unlimited practice while your college's subscription is active.
  • Added by your college: if your faculty or college admin created your account, sign in with the temporary password you were given. You will be asked to change it.

Your first sign-in

  1. Accept the Privacy Policy, Terms of Service and Conditions of Use.
  2. Read and accept the ethical-use agreement. Free-trial users also accept the trial rules.
  3. Open your start page: My assignments for students, a dashboard for staff, or Modules for free-trial users.

Experiment user guide

Every module has the same three tabs:

  1. Task Description: the aim, principle, apparatus and procedure for that experiment.
  2. Experiment: the interactive bench. Set up the apparatus, give the simulated drugs and watch the responses.
  3. Observation: the observation sheet, with results and statistics, and the viva questions that check your understanding.

Each module's own instructions are in its Task Description tab. While you work, the Guide button in the module's status bar opens this page at that module, in a new tab, so it does not end your visit. It is hidden during exams.

Faculty guide

  1. Create a batch. Each batch gets its own class code, which students can use to join.
  2. Enrol students. Upload a CSV or Excel list, add students one at a time, or let them join with the code. Credentials for uploaded students can be downloaded once.
  3. Assign an experiment. Set the opening and due dates, attempts and maximum marks, and whether late work is accepted.
  4. Review submissions. Each student's score starts as provisional marks. You can override it and add feedback, and Save & next moves you through the queue.
  5. Export marks. The marks sheet shows each student's best attempt and exports to Excel or CSV.

How the automatic score is worked out

Viva 40%, correct unknown or numeric answer 30% (only for experiments that have one) and completed observation sheet 30%, scaled to the assignment's maximum marks.

Exam mode

A countdown starts when the student opens the experiment. It keeps running if they close the tab, and the attempt is submitted automatically at zero. Reference datasets, auto-run and "skip waiting" shortcuts, answer checks and viva marking are all hidden until submission.

The answer check runs in the student's browser, so a student with technical knowledge could read the true value from the page code. Your review remains the final authority, and each student gets their own randomised unknowns.

Lab assistants

Lab assistants can see batches and submissions, enrol students and reset student passwords. They cannot assign, grade or export marks.

Institutional guide

  • Staff: college admins add or deactivate faculty, lab assistants and other admins, and reset passwords.
  • Experiments: switch individual experiments on or off for your college.
  • College settings: set your college's header line and grievance officer.
  • Data requests: handle correction and deletion requests from your users under Requests.
  • Audit log: review sign-ins, account changes, uploads, assignments, grading and exports.
  • Subscriptions: if your college's subscription lapses, its students fall back to one visit per module. To subscribe, write to {{ SITE_CREATOR_EMAIL }}.

3Rs & ethics

  • Replacement: use methods that avoid animals, such as computer simulations, wherever they achieve the learning objective.
  • Reduction: where animals are unavoidable, use the fewest needed for a valid result, through good design and statistics.
  • Refinement: modify procedures, housing and care to minimise pain and distress and to improve welfare.

Simulated results are generated by models for learning. They are not real data, and XPRISM does not replace the approvals required for real research involving animals. Read the full ethical use of animals text, including the CCSEA and PCI framework.

FAQs

What does the free trial include?

You can explore every enabled module once, free of charge. Trial work is not saved or submitted to any faculty.

When does a free-trial visit end?

When you go back to the lab index, the home page or a dashboard, open another experiment, sign out, or after {{ TRIAL_HOURS }} hours. Reloading the same page is fine. After that, the module is available by subscription only.

Can I browse the modules without an account?

Yes. Anyone can read the module catalogue and this documentation. Opening a simulation needs an account.

How do I get unlimited access?

Through your college's subscription, or an individual subscription. Write to {{ SITE_CREATOR_EMAIL }}.

What do I need to run a simulation?

A current desktop browser with JavaScript turned on. The simulations run in your browser, so nothing needs to be installed.

I've forgotten my password or my account is locked.

Reset your password by e-mail, or ask your faculty. An account locks for a short time after repeated failed sign-ins.

Can I see or delete my data?

Yes. My data lets you download what is held about you and request correction or deletion. See the Privacy Policy.
