MODULE 20 Examination 20: To Study the Analgesic Activity of a Given Drug by the Tail-Flick Method (Analgesiometer) in Rats
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Analgesic Activity by the Tail-Flick Method (D'Amour & Smith)

Measure the tail-flick latency of rats when radiant heat from a nichrome wire (heated by a current of 5–6 A) is applied about 2 cm from the tip of the tail, before and 30, 60 and 90 min after the test drug. Opioid analgesics such as morphine prolong the latency. A 10-second cut-off prevents tissue damage: if the tail has not flicked by 10 s the heat is switched off at once and 10 s is recorded.

1

Principle

The tail flick is a spinal reflex to a noxious thermal stimulus, modulated by descending pathways from the brainstem. Opioids act at spinal and supraspinal μ (and κ) receptors and raise the latency. Peripherally acting NSAIDs have little effect on this acute heat pain. The latency depends on the heat intensity (current) and on where the heat falls on the tail, so both must be kept constant.

2

Response & Cut-off

Place the rat in the restrainer with its tail over the wire. Switch on the heat (the timer starts) and press STOP at the sudden flick of the tail away from the wire. Small twitches are not the end-point. Switch off the heat at 10 s if there is no flick and record 10 s. Heating beyond the cut-off burns the tail skin, and later readings from that rat become invalid.

3

Calculation

% Maximum possible effect (%MPE) = (Test latency − Basal latency) ÷ (Cut-off − Basal latency) × 100. Express each group as mean ± SEM and compare the test group with the vehicle group at each time point (unpaired t-test, n = 6, df = 10).

Requirements

  • Analgesiometer (nichrome wire, ammeter 0–10 A, intensity control, reaction timer) and a perspex rat restrainer
  • 12 albino rats, 150–200 g (screened: basal tail-flick latency 2–5 s), permanent marker for tail numbers
  • Top-pan balance (1 g) with a plastic container; analytical balance (0.1 mg), volumetric flasks 5/10/25 mL
  • Morphine sulphate 5 mg/kg s.c., Pentazocine 10 mg/kg i.p., Tramadol 20 mg/kg i.p., Aspirin 100 mg/kg p.o. (suspended in 1% CMC), Naloxone 1 mg/kg s.c. (given with morphine)
  • Normal saline, 1% CMC; dose volume 1 mL/kg for injections and 10 mL/kg by mouth
  • 1 mL syringes, 26 G needles, oral feeding (gavage) needle

Procedure

  1. Weigh each rat in the tared container, record its weight and number its tail with a marker.
  2. Allocate the 12 rats to Group I (vehicle) and Group II (test) at random by drawing lots.
  3. Prepare the drug solution: concentration (mg/mL) = dose (mg/kg) ÷ dose volume (mL/kg); weigh, make up to volume in a volumetric flask and mix.
  4. Switch on the analgesiometer and set the current to 5–6 A.
  5. Put the rat in the restrainer and slide it so the wire lies about 2 cm from the tail tip.
  6. Switch on the heat and stop the timer at the tail flick. Record the basal latency of each rat.
  7. Calculate each rat's dose volume and give vehicle (Group I) or test drug (Group II) by the correct route; note the time of dosing.
  8. Repeat the readings 30, 60 and 90 min after each rat's own dosing time, then calculate %MPE, mean ± SEM and the t-test.
Ethics & 3Rs: the protocol must be approved by the IAEC under CCSEA guidelines. Use the minimum number of animals (n = 6 per group), screen out rats with a basal latency outside 2–5 s, never exceed the cut-off, keep restraint as short as possible and return each rat to its home cage after testing.
