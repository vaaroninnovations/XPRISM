MODULE 20 Examination 20: To Study the Analgesic Activity of a Given Drug by the Tail-Flick Method (Analgesiometer) in Rats
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Analgesic Activity by the Tail-Flick Method (D'Amour & Smith)

Measure the tail-flick latency of rats when radiant heat from a nichrome wire (heated by a current of 5–6 A) is applied about 2 cm from the tip of the tail, before and 30, 60 and 90 min after the test drug. Opioid analgesics such as morphine prolong the latency. A 10-second cut-off prevents tissue damage.

1

Principle

The tail flick is a spinal reflex to a noxious thermal stimulus, modulated by descending pathways from the brainstem. Opioids act at spinal and supraspinal μ (and κ) receptors and raise the latency. Peripherally acting NSAIDs have little effect on this acute heat pain.

2

Response & Cut-off

Place the rat in the restrainer with its tail over the wire. Switch on the heat (the timer starts) and press STOP at the sudden flick of the tail away from the wire. Small twitches are not the end-point. Stop the heat at 10 s if there is no flick and record 10 s.

3

Calculation

% Maximum possible effect (%MPE) = (Test latency − Basal latency) ÷ (Cut-off − Basal latency) × 100. Compare the test group with the vehicle group at each time point (unpaired t-test, n = 6).

Requirements

  • Analgesiometer (nichrome wire, ammeter 0–10 A, intensity control, reaction timer) and a perspex rat restrainer
  • Albino rats, 150–200 g (screened: basal tail-flick latency 2–5 s)
  • Morphine sulphate 5 mg/kg s.c., Pentazocine 10 mg/kg i.p., Tramadol 20 mg/kg i.p., Aspirin 100 mg/kg p.o., Naloxone 1 mg/kg s.c.
  • 1 mL syringes, 26 G needles, oral feeding tube

Procedure

  1. Switch on the analgesiometer and set the current to 5–6 A.
  2. Put the rat in the restrainer and slide it so the wire lies about 2 cm from the tail tip.
  3. Switch on the heat and stop the timer at the tail flick. Record the basal latency of each rat.
  4. Give vehicle (Group I) or test drug (Group II).
  5. Repeat the readings at 30, 60 and 90 min, then calculate %MPE and compare the groups.
