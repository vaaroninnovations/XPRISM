MODULE 17 Examination 17: To Estimate the Strength of an Unknown Histamine Solution on Guinea-Pig Ileum by the Four-Point Bioassay
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Four-Point (2 + 2) Bioassay of Histamine on Guinea-Pig Ileum

Choose two doses of standard (s1, s2) and two doses of test (t1, t2) with the same dose ratio d (s2/s1 = t2/t1 = 2). All four responses must lie on the linear part of the log-DRC. Give them in a 4 × 4 Latin-square order on a fixed time cycle, check that the standard and test lines are parallel, and calculate the potency of the test. You carry out the whole practical yourself: prepare the Tyrode solution and the histamine standard, isolate the guinea-pig ileum, set up the 37 °C organ bath, lever and kymograph, equilibrate the tissue, give every dose with a tuberculin syringe on a regular time cycle, wash, measure each contraction with the ruler and calculate the strength of the unknown from your own readings. Mistakes in technique show up in your record just as they would at the bench.

1

Principle

If test and standard contain the same active substance, their log dose–response lines are parallel. The horizontal distance between the two lines is the log of the potency ratio M.

2

Formula

log M = [(T1 + T2) − (S1 + S2)] / [(S2 − S1) + (T2 − T1)] × log d
Conc. of test = antilog(log M) × (s1 / t1) × conc. of standard

3

Advantages & time cycle

More precise than the 3-point assay. The slopes (S2 − S1) and (T2 − T1) can be compared to test parallelism, which validates the assay. The Latin square cancels sequence and drift effects. Contact 30 s, two washes, next dose 3 min after the last, every time.

Requirements & Tyrode solution (per litre)

  • Guinea pig 300–500 g, fasted overnight (water allowed), humanely killed by trained animal-house staff (off-screen)
  • Student organ bath (20 mL) in a 37 °C water jacket with thermostat, aerator, isotonic frontal lever (0.5 g load), Sherrington kymograph
  • Histamine acid phosphate stock ≡ 1 mg/mL histamine base; unknown histamine solution “X”
Sodium chloride (NaCl)8.0 g
Potassium chloride (KCl)0.2 g
Magnesium chloride (MgCl2)0.1 g
Sodium dihydrogen phosphate (NaH2PO4)0.05 g
Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3)1.0 g
Glucose1.0 g
Calcium chloride, anhydrous (CaCl2)0.2 g

Prepare 2 litres. Dissolve everything except CaCl2 in about 1.8 L of distilled water, add CaCl2 last (otherwise calcium carbonate/phosphate precipitates), make up to 2000 mL and mix.

Procedure

  1. Prepare 2 L Tyrode. Dilute histamine 1 mg/mL to 10 µg/mL (tube 1) and then to the 1 µg/mL standard (tube 2) with Tyrode.
  2. Confirm death, pin the animal, open the abdomen and find the ileocaecal junction. Discard the terminal 10 cm; excise the ileum above it into warm aerated Tyrode.
  3. Flush the lumen, cut a 2–3 cm segment and tie threads to both ends.
  4. Heat the jacket to 37 °C, fill the bath (20 mL), aerate, mount the ileum (0.5 g load, lever level, light contact), slowest drum speed.
  5. Equilibrate 30 min, washing every 10 min.
  6. Pilot: standard 0.05–0.8 mL and one or two volumes of X to find the linear range.
  7. Fix s1, s2 = 2 × s1 and t1, t2 = 2 × t1 so that T1 ≈ S1 and T2 ≈ S2. Give the 16 doses in the Latin-square order shown, every 3 min.
  8. Measure every height, find the means, apply the formula and compare the slopes of the two lines.
