MODULE 21 Examination 21: To Study the Anti-inflammatory Activity of a Given Drug by Carrageenan-Induced Paw Oedema in Rats
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Acute Anti-inflammatory Activity: Carrageenan-Induced Rat Paw Oedema (Winter et al., 1962)

Inject 0.1 mL of 1% w/v carrageenan under the plantar skin of the right hind paw and measure the paw volume with a plethysmometer before and 1, 2, 3 and 4 h afterwards. The test drug is given orally 1 h before carrageenan. An anti-inflammatory drug reduces the oedema compared with the vehicle group.

You do the whole practical yourself: identify and weigh 12 rats, allocate them to two groups by drawing lots, calculate and prepare the drug suspension and the 1% carrageenan, set up and zero the plethysmometer, record the basal paw volumes, dose every rat, inject the carrageenan on time, take every reading by dipping the paw exactly to the ink mark, write the readings in your notebook and work out the mean oedema and % inhibition. Errors of technique (wrong dose, wrong site, wrong timing, a careless dip) appear in your data just as they would in the laboratory.

1

Principle: a biphasic oedema

Early phase (0–1.5 h): histamine, 5-HT and bradykinin. Late phase (2.5–4 h): prostaglandins from inducible COX-2, with neutrophil infiltration. NSAIDs mainly inhibit the late phase; corticosteroids inhibit both; antihistamine/anti-5-HT drugs inhibit only the early phase.

2

Plethysmometer technique

The paw is marked with ink at the lateral malleolus. Each time, dip it into the measuring cell exactly up to the mark. The displaced fluid is read in mL; press the HOLD pedal to capture the reading. Fill the cell with water containing a wetting agent and zero the instrument before use.

3

Calculation

Oedema = Vt − V0 (mL).
% Inhibition = (1 − Etest ÷ Econtrol) × 100, using the mean oedema at each time point. Compare the groups by unpaired t-test (n = 6).
Dose volume (mL) = dose (mg/kg) × body wt (kg) ÷ concentration (mg/mL).

Requirements

  • Digital plethysmometer (water cell with wetting agent), HOLD foot pedal
  • Albino rats, 150–200 g, fasted overnight with water ad libitum; picric acid and a marker pen for identification
  • Carrageenan 1% w/v in normal saline (0.1 mL per paw), 1 mL tuberculin syringe with 26 G needle
  • Indomethacin 10 mg/kg p.o., Diclofenac 10 mg/kg p.o., Aspirin 100 mg/kg p.o., Paracetamol 100 mg/kg p.o., Dexamethasone 1 mg/kg s.c., Cyproheptadine 10 mg/kg p.o. (oral drugs suspended in 0.5% CMC, 10 mL/kg; dexamethasone in saline, 1 mL/kg)
  • Analytical and animal balances, mortar and pestle, measuring cylinder, magnetic stirrer, 3 mL syringe with ball-tipped gavage needle

Procedure

  1. Mark each rat (picric acid code), draw an ink line at the lateral malleolus of the right hind paw, weigh it and allocate it to Group I or II by drawing a lot (n = 6 each).
  2. Prepare the drug suspension (triturate with 0.5% CMC) and 1% carrageenan in saline (stir until fully hydrated).
  3. Fill the plethysmometer cell, switch it on and press ZERO with the cell empty of any paw.
  4. Measure the basal volume (V0) of the right hind paw of each rat.
  5. Give vehicle (Group I) or the test drug (Group II) orally.
  6. After 1 h inject 0.1 mL of 1% carrageenan sub-plantar into the right hind paw.
  7. Measure the paw volume at 1, 2, 3 and 4 h; calculate oedema and % inhibition.

At the end of the study the animals are humanely euthanised off-screen by trained staff, as approved by the IAEC and per CCSEA guidelines.
