MODULE 19 Examination 19: To Study the Analgesic Activity of a Given Drug Using Eddy's Hot Plate Method in Mice
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Central Analgesic Activity by Eddy's Hot Plate Method

Measure the reaction time (latency to hind-paw licking or jumping) of mice placed on a hot plate kept at 55 ± 0.5 °C, before and 30, 60 and 90 min after the test drug. Centrally acting analgesics such as morphine prolong the reaction time. A 15-second cut-off prevents tissue injury: a mouse that has not responded by 15 s is lifted off the plate at once and 15 s is recorded.

1

Principle

The heated plate is a thermal stimulus. Paw licking and jumping are supraspinally integrated responses, so the test is selective for centrally acting (opioid) analgesics. Peripherally acting NSAIDs such as aspirin have little effect. Latency falls as plate temperature rises, so the plate must be stable at the set temperature before every reading.

2

Response & Cut-off

Start the stopwatch the moment the mouse's paws touch the plate. Stop it at the first hind-paw lick or jump. Early signs are shifting from paw to paw and lifting or licking the forepaws (not the end-point). Remove any mouse that has not responded by 15 s and record 15 s. Leaving a mouse on the plate beyond the cut-off is a welfare breach and damages the paws, which makes its later readings invalid.

3

Calculation

% Maximum possible effect (%MPE) = (Test latency − Basal latency) ÷ (Cut-off − Basal latency) × 100. Express each group as mean ± SEM and compare the test group with the vehicle group at each time point (unpaired t-test, n = 6, df = 10).

Requirements

  • Eddy's hot plate with thermostat (55 ± 0.5 °C) and a transparent acrylic cylinder
  • 12 albino mice, 20–25 g (screened: basal reaction time 3–8 s), picric acid solution for marking
  • Top-pan balance (0.1 g) with a plastic beaker; analytical balance (0.1 mg), volumetric flasks 5/10/25 mL
  • Morphine sulphate 5 mg/kg s.c., Pentazocine 10 mg/kg s.c., Tramadol 20 mg/kg i.p., Aspirin 100 mg/kg p.o. (suspended in 1% CMC), Naloxone 1 mg/kg s.c. (given with morphine)
  • Normal saline (0.9% NaCl), 1% CMC; dose volume 10 mL/kg for every route in mice
  • Stopwatch, 1 mL tuberculin syringes with 26 G needles, oral feeding (gavage) needle

Procedure

  1. Take each mouse from the stock cage, weigh it in the tared beaker, record the weight and mark it with picric acid.
  2. Allocate the 12 marked mice to Group I (vehicle) and Group II (test) at random by drawing lots.
  3. Prepare the drug solution: concentration (mg/mL) = dose (mg/kg) ÷ dose volume (mL/kg). Weigh the drug, transfer to a volumetric flask, make up to the mark with the vehicle and mix.
  4. Switch on the hot plate, set it to 55 °C and let it stabilise.
  5. Record the basal reaction time of every mouse (stopwatch START on contact, STOP at the end-point, reset between mice).
  6. Calculate each mouse's dose volume (mL = weight in kg × 10 mL/kg) and give vehicle (Group I) or test drug (Group II) by the correct route; note the time of dosing.
  7. Record the reaction time again 30, 60 and 90 min after each mouse's own dosing time.
  8. Calculate %MPE, mean ± SEM and the t-test, and draw your conclusion.
Ethics & 3Rs: the protocol must be approved by the IAEC under CCSEA guidelines. Use the minimum number of animals (n = 6 per group), screen out animals with a basal latency outside 3–8 s, never exceed the cut-off and return each animal to its home cage after testing. Opioid-treated mice are observed until recovery by the animal-house staff.
