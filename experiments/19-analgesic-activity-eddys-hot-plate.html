MODULE 19 Examination 19: To Study the Analgesic Activity of a Given Drug Using Eddy's Hot Plate Method in Mice
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Central Analgesic Activity by Eddy's Hot Plate Method

Measure the reaction time (latency to hind-paw licking or jumping) of mice placed on a hot plate kept at 55 ± 0.5 °C, before and 30, 60 and 90 min after the test drug. Centrally acting analgesics such as morphine prolong the reaction time. A 15-second cut-off prevents tissue injury.

1

Principle

The heated plate is a thermal stimulus. Paw licking and jumping are supraspinally integrated responses, so the test is selective for centrally acting (opioid) analgesics. Peripherally acting NSAIDs such as aspirin have little effect.

2

Response & Cut-off

Start the stopwatch when the mouse touches the plate. Stop it at the first hind-paw lick or jump. Early signs are shifting from paw to paw and lifting the forepaws. Remove any mouse that has not responded by 15 s and record 15 s.

3

Calculation

% Maximum possible effect (%MPE) = (Test latency − Basal latency) ÷ (Cut-off − Basal latency) × 100. Compare the test group with the vehicle group at each time point (unpaired t-test, n = 6).

Requirements

  • Eddy's hot plate with thermostat (55 ± 0.5 °C) and a transparent acrylic cylinder
  • Albino mice, 20–25 g (screened: basal reaction time 3–8 s)
  • Morphine sulphate 5 mg/kg s.c., Pentazocine 10 mg/kg s.c., Tramadol 20 mg/kg i.p., Aspirin 100 mg/kg p.o., Naloxone 1 mg/kg s.c.
  • Stopwatch, 1 mL syringes

Procedure

  1. Switch on the hot plate and let it stabilise at 55 °C.
  2. Record the basal reaction time of each mouse.
  3. Give vehicle (Group I) or test drug (Group II).
  4. Record the reaction time again at 30, 60 and 90 min.
  5. Calculate %MPE and compare the groups.
