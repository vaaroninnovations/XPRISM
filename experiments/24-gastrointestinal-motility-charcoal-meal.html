MODULE 24 Examination 24: To Study the Effect of Atropine and Metoclopramide on Gastrointestinal Transit in Mice by the Charcoal Meal Test
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Effect of Drugs on Gastrointestinal Transit: Charcoal Meal Test

Give mice a charcoal meal by mouth after pretreatment with saline, atropine (antimuscarinic, inhibits motility) or metoclopramide (D2 antagonist / 5-HT4 agonist prokinetic). After 30 min, remove the small intestine, lay it straight and measure the distance travelled by the charcoal front as a percentage of the total length of the small intestine.

You do every step yourself: take fasted mice, mark them with picric acid, weigh them and allocate them by drawing lots; calculate and prepare the atropine and metoclopramide solutions and the charcoal meal; inject each pretreatment i.p.; give the charcoal meal by feeding needle 30 min later; end the transit exactly 30 min after the meal; then cut out the small intestine at the pylorus and the ileocaecal junction, lay it straight beside the scale, read the charcoal front and the total length, and analyse your own numbers. Mistakes (fed mice, wrong dose or route, wrong intervals, an unshaken suspension, wrong cut points, stretching the gut, mis-reading the scale) show up in your results.

1

Principle

Activated charcoal is not absorbed and is easily seen through the gut wall. The distance it travels in a fixed time is an index of propulsive (peristaltic) activity.

2

Calculation

% transit = distance moved by charcoal ÷ total length (pylorus → ileocaecal junction) × 100

% change = (Tdrug − Tcontrol) ÷ Tcontrol × 100

3

Expected

Atropine reduces transit (blocks ACh at M₃ on gut smooth muscle). Metoclopramide increases transit (enhances ACh release from myenteric neurons).

Requirements

  • Mice 20–30 g, 6 per group, fasted 18 h (water ad lib)
  • Atropine sulphate 5 mg/kg i.p.; metoclopramide 10 mg/kg i.p.; saline 10 mL/kg i.p.
  • Charcoal meal: 10% activated charcoal in 5% gum acacia, 0.3 mL per mouse
  • 1 mL syringes with 26 G needles and a 20 G ball-tipped oral feeding needle
  • Picric acid, animal and analytical balances, 10 mL volumetric flasks / stoppered cylinder
  • Dissection tray, fine scissors, moist glass plate, 60 cm scale

Procedure

  1. Mark, weigh and randomise 18 fasted mice into 3 groups of 6. Prepare atropine 0.5 mg/mL and metoclopramide 1 mg/mL in saline (10 mL/kg) and the charcoal meal.
  2. Inject saline or drug i.p.
  3. After 30 min give 0.3 mL charcoal meal orally (shake the suspension before drawing).
  4. 30 min later the mice are humanely killed by trained staff (off-screen, as approved by the IAEC and per CCSEA) and the small intestine is removed from pylorus to caecum.
  5. Lay it straight without stretching. Measure the total length and the distance to the charcoal front.
  6. Calculate % transit; compare groups by t-test.
