MODULE 23 Examination 23: To Evaluate the Anti-ulcer and Antisecretory Activity of Ranitidine and Omeprazole by the Pylorus Ligation (Shay Rat) Model
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Anti-ulcer Activity by Pylorus Ligation (Shay Rat Model)

Ligation of the pylorus in fasted rats causes accumulation of acid–pepsin in the stomach and ulceration of the forestomach and glandular mucosa within hours. Pretreat groups with vehicle, ranitidine (H2 blocker) or omeprazole (proton-pump inhibitor), ligate the pylorus, and after 4 h measure gastric volume, pH, free and total acidity and the ulcer index.

1

Acidity by titration

Titrate 1 mL of centrifuged gastric juice with 0.01 N NaOH: Töpfer's reagent (red → yellow) gives free acidity; then phenolphthalein (colourless → pink) gives total acidity.
Acidity (mEq/L) = VNaOH × N × 1000 ÷ Vsample

2

Ulcer score

0 normal • 0.5 red colouration • 1 spot ulcers • 1.5 haemorrhagic streaks • 2 ulcers 3–5 mm • 3 ulcers > 5 mm. Ulcer index = mean score of the group.
% protection = (UIc − UIt) / UIc × 100

3

Expected

Both drugs reduce volume and acidity, raise pH and protect against ulcers. Omeprazole (irreversible H⁺/K⁺-ATPase block) is usually more effective than ranitidine.

Requirements

  • Wistar rats 150–200 g, 6 per group, fasted 24 h (water ad lib) in wire-bottom cages
  • Ranitidine 50 mg/kg p.o., omeprazole 20 mg/kg p.o., vehicle (1% CMC)
  • Ether / ketamine anaesthesia, silk thread, surgical kit
  • Centrifuge, pH meter, burette, 0.01 N NaOH, Töpfer's reagent, phenolphthalein
  • Magnifying lens (10×) for ulcer scoring

Procedure

  1. Fast the rats for 24 h. Give vehicle / drug orally.
  2. After 30 min, under light anaesthesia, open the abdomen and ligate the pylorus without damaging the blood supply. Close the wound.
  3. After 4 h, sacrifice the rats, ligate the oesophagus, remove the stomach and collect its contents.
  4. Measure volume and pH; centrifuge; titrate 1 mL of supernatant for free and total acidity.
  5. Open the stomach along the greater curvature, rinse, and score ulcers under a lens.
