MODULE 10 Examination 10: To Study Haloperidol-Induced Catalepsy (Bar Test) and Amphetamine-Induced Stereotypy in Rats, and their Modification by Drugs
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Dopamine-Related Motor Behaviour: Catalepsy and Stereotypy

Part A: haloperidol (1 mg/kg i.p.) blocks striatal D2 receptors and makes rats cataleptic: they keep an imposed posture. Measure how long each rat keeps its forepaws on a horizontal bar and test whether a drug reverses the catalepsy. Part B: amphetamine (5 mg/kg i.p.) releases striatal dopamine and causes stereotyped sniffing, licking and gnawing. Score the behaviour and test whether a drug antagonises it.

A

Bar test for catalepsy

Gently place both forepaws of the rat on a steel bar 9 cm above the base, hind paws on the base. Start the stopwatch and stop it when the rat removes both forepaws (descent latency). Cut-off 180 s. Readings before and 30, 60, 90 and 120 min after haloperidol. Anticholinergics (scopolamine, trihexyphenidyl) and amantadine reduce catalepsy: a model of drug-induced parkinsonism and its treatment.

B

Stereotypy scoring (0–4)

Observe each rat in a perspex cage and score: 0 normal activity; 1 discontinuous sniffing with exploration; 2 continuous sniffing, periodic exploration; 3 continuous sniffing with discontinuous licking/gnawing, brief locomotion; 4 continuous licking/gnawing, no exploration. D2 antagonists (haloperidol, chlorpromazine) block it: a screen for antipsychotic activity.

Requirements

  • Bar test apparatus (steel bar 0.9 cm diameter, 9 cm high), stopwatch
  • Perspex observation cage, albino rats 150–200 g
  • Haloperidol 1 mg/kg i.p.; Scopolamine 1 mg/kg i.p.; Trihexyphenidyl 5 mg/kg i.p.; Amantadine 40 mg/kg i.p.; Diazepam 2 mg/kg i.p.
  • d-Amphetamine 5 mg/kg i.p.; Chlorpromazine 3 mg/kg i.p.; Propranolol 10 mg/kg i.p.

Calculation

  • Part A: mean descent latency (s) ± SEM at each time; % reversal = (1 − Test ÷ Control) × 100; unpaired t-test.
  • Part B: scores are ordinal: report median (and mean ± SEM); % inhibition of mean score; Mann–Whitney U test.
  • Group I receives the inducer + vehicle, Group II the inducer + test drug (n = 6).
