MODULE 15 Examination 15: To Estimate the Strength of an Unknown Histamine Solution on Guinea-Pig Ileum by Interpolation and Bracketing
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Bioassay of Histamine by Interpolation and Bracketing

Construct a log dose–response curve of standard histamine (1 µg/mL) on the guinea-pig ileum. Give a fixed volume of the unknown test solution and interpolate its response on the standard curve. Then confirm the estimate by bracketing: give the test dose between two standard doses, one producing a slightly smaller and the other a slightly larger response.

1

Interpolation

Plot height against log dose of standard. Draw a horizontal line from the test height to the curve and drop a perpendicular to read the equivalent standard dose. Conc. of test = equivalent dose ÷ test volume.

2

Bracketing

Sequence S1 → T → S2 with S1 < T < S2 in height. Then
log x = log s1 + (T − S1)/(S2 − S1) × log(s2/s1)
where x is the standard dose equivalent to the test dose.

3

Why bracket?

Tissue sensitivity changes slowly. Responses given close in time share the same sensitivity, so bracketing is less affected by drift than reading off a curve recorded much earlier.

Requirements

  • Guinea-pig terminal ileum, Tyrode 37 °C, aerated, 20 mL bath
  • Isotonic frontal writing lever, kymograph
  • Standard histamine 1 µg/mL; test solution of unknown strength
  • Semi-log graph paper

Procedure

  1. Record standard responses (0.05–0.8 µg), 30-s contact, 3-min cycle.
  2. Give two volumes of test whose responses fall in the linear range; interpolate.
  3. Choose standard doses s1 and s2 whose responses bracket the test response.
  4. Give S1 → T → S2 (and repeat, reversing the order S2 → T → S1).
  5. Calculate the strength by both methods and compare.
