MODULE 22 Examination 22: To Record the Effects of Autonomic Drugs and Histamine on Blood Pressure and Heart Rate of the Anaesthetised Rat (Dale's Vasomotor Reversal)
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Effects of Drugs on Blood Pressure and Heart Rate: Dale's Vasomotor Reversal

Record carotid arterial pressure and heart rate in a urethane-anaesthetised rat. Inject adrenaline, noradrenaline, isoprenaline, acetylcholine and histamine intravenously, and repeat after atropine, the α-blocker phentolamine and then propranolol. Demonstrate adrenaline reversal (pressor → depressor after α-blockade) and the nicotinic pressor action of a large dose of ACh after atropine.

1

Catecholamines

NA (α ≫ β₂): ↑ SBP & DBP, reflex bradycardia. Adrenaline (α + β₁ + β₂): ↑ SBP, smaller ↑ DBP, followed by a slight dip. Isoprenaline (β only): ↓ DBP & MAP, marked tachycardia.

2

Dale's reversal

After an α-blocker, adrenaline's α-vasoconstriction is removed and its β₂-vasodilation is unmasked, so adrenaline now lowers BP. NA rise is greatly reduced; isoprenaline is unchanged.

3

ACh reversal

ACh (muscarinic) causes a brief fall in BP and HR. Atropine abolishes it. A large dose of ACh after atropine then raises BP by stimulating sympathetic ganglia and the adrenal medulla (nicotinic).

Requirements

  • Rat 200–250 g, urethane 1.25 g/kg i.p.
  • Tracheal cannula; carotid cannula (heparinised saline) to pressure transducer / mercury manometer; jugular or femoral vein cannula
  • Polygraph / data acquisition system
  • Adrenaline, NA (2 µg/kg), isoprenaline (1 µg/kg), ACh (1 and 50 µg/kg), histamine (2 µg/kg)
  • Atropine 1 mg/kg, propranolol 1 mg/kg, phentolamine 2 mg/kg, mepyramine 2 mg/kg

Procedure

  1. Record a stable baseline for 10 min.
  2. Inject each agonist (0.1 mL, flushed with 0.2 mL saline). Wait for BP and HR to return to baseline (≈ 3 min) before the next dose.
  3. Give atropine; repeat ACh 1 µg/kg, then ACh 50 µg/kg.
  4. Give phentolamine; repeat adrenaline (reversal), NA and isoprenaline.
  5. Finally give propranolol; repeat isoprenaline and adrenaline (both responses now abolished).
  6. Measure peak changes in SBP, DBP, MAP and HR. MAP = DBP + (SBP − DBP)/3.
