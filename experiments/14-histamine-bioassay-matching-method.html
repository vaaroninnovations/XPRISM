MODULE 14 Examination 14: To Estimate the Strength of an Unknown Histamine Solution on Guinea-Pig Ileum by the Matching Method
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Bioassay of Histamine by the Matching (Bracketing-by-Match) Method

Record contractions of the isolated guinea-pig ileum to graded doses of a standard histamine solution (1 µg/mL) that you prepare yourself. Then give doses of the test solution of unknown strength and adjust the test volume until its response exactly matches a standard response. Equal responses mean equal amounts of histamine, so the strength of the test can be calculated. You do every step: make the Tyrode and the dilutions, isolate the ileum, set up the warmed organ bath, lever and kymograph, give each dose with the syringe, time it, wash, measure each contraction with the ruler and do the calculation. The strength of the unknown is different for every attempt.

1

Principle

Histamine contracts ileal smooth muscle through H1 receptors (Gq → IP3 → Ca2+). The guinea-pig ileum is very sensitive, has little spontaneous activity and gives quick, reproducible contractions. Within the sub-maximal range the height is graded with dose, and two doses that give identical heights contain the same quantity of histamine.

2

Calculation

If x mL of test matches y µg of standard, then x mL of test contains y µg of histamine:
Conc. of test = y / x µg/mL.
Example: 0.25 mL test matches 0.4 µg standard → 0.4 ÷ 0.25 = 1.6 µg/mL.

3

Good practice

Work in the steep, linear part of the DRC (20–80% of max). Keep a fixed time cycle: 30 s contact, wash twice, next dose 3 min after the last. Confirm a match by repeating it (S → T → S), because tissue sensitivity drifts slowly. Atropine in the Tyrode removes cholinergic spasms; 32–35 °C damps spontaneous movement.

Requirements

  • Guinea pig (300–500 g), fasted overnight with water; killed humanely by the animal-house staff (CCSEA guidelines)
  • Tyrode solution (you prepare 2 L), aerated; jacketed organ bath 20 mL at 32–37 °C with thermostatic circulator
  • Isotonic frontal writing lever (load 0.5 g, magnification ≈ 6×), Sherrington kymograph, smoked paper
  • Histamine acid phosphate stock ≡ 1 mg/mL histamine base; tuberculin syringe; stopwatch
  • Test solution of histamine (unknown strength); atropine sulphate 1 mg/mL (optional)

Tyrode solution (per litre)

Sodium chloride (NaCl)8.0 g
Potassium chloride (KCl)0.2 g
Magnesium chloride (MgCl2)0.1 g
Sodium dihydrogen phosphate (NaH2PO4)0.05 g
Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3)1.0 g
Glucose1.0 g
Calcium chloride, anhydrous (CaCl2), added last0.2 g

Dissolve everything except CaCl2 in about 1.8 L distilled water, add CaCl2 last (otherwise calcium carbonate/phosphate precipitates), make up to 2000 mL and mix.

Procedure

  1. Prepare 2 L Tyrode. Dilute histamine stock 1 in 100 (10 µg/mL, tube 1) and then 1 in 10 to give the standard 1 µg/mL (tube 2), using C1V1 = C2V2.
  2. Confirm death, pin the animal, open the abdomen in the midline and lift the caecum aside to find the ileocaecal junction.
  3. Remove ~10 cm of terminal ileum (discard the 1 cm next to the junction) into Tyrode. Trim the mesentery, flush the lumen gently until clear, cut a 2–3 cm piece and tie a thread to each end.
  4. Fill the reservoir (with atropine if used), warm the jacket to 32–37 °C, fill the bath to 20 mL, aerate gently, smoke the paper and set a slow drum speed.
  5. Tie the ileum to the holder, immerse it, tie the upper thread to the lever, load 0.5 g, level the lever and let the point touch the paper lightly. Equilibrate 30 min, washing every 10 min.
  6. Give standard doses 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8 µg (0.05–0.8 mL of 1 µg/mL): drum on, inject, 30 s contact, drain and refill twice, drum off, next dose 3 min after the previous one.
  7. Give a test dose (e.g. 0.2 mL). Compare its height with the standards and change the test volume (or the standard dose) until the two heights match within ±5%.
  8. Repeat the matched pair (S → T → S) to confirm. Measure every height with the ruler, enter dose and height in the notebook, and calculate the strength of the test.
