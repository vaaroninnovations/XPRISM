MODULE 07 Examination 07: To Study the Effect of Acetylcholine and Atropine on the Ciliary Motility of Frog Oesophagus
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Effect of Drugs on Ciliary Motility of the Frog Oesophagus

The ciliated epithelium of the frog's buccal cavity and oesophagus carries particles towards the stomach. Place a poppy seed at the oral end of the opened oesophagus and measure the time it takes to travel a fixed distance (20 mm) in frog Ringer (control), after acetylcholine, after atropine, and after acetylcholine in the presence of atropine.

1

Principle

Ciliary beating and mucus secretion in the frog oesophagus are increased by muscarinic (M) stimulation. Faster beating moves the seed faster, so the travel time falls.

2

Expected

ACh: time ↓ (motility ↑). Physostigmine: time ↓ (more endogenous ACh). Atropine: time ↑ slightly and it blocks the ACh effect.

3

Measure

Start the stop-watch when the seed crosses the start mark and stop it at the finish mark. Velocity (mm/s) = 20 ÷ time. Take 3 readings per treatment.

Requirements

  • Frog (Rana tigrina), pithed (brain and spinal cord destroyed)
  • Cork board, pins, frog Ringer solution, dropper
  • Poppy seeds, stop-watch, scale (marks 20 mm apart)
  • Acetylcholine 100 µg/mL, physostigmine 1 mg/mL, atropine sulphate 1 mg/mL

Procedure

  1. Pith the frog, cut the lower jaw and open the oesophagus to the stomach; pin it on the board, mucosa upwards.
  2. Keep the tissue moist with Ringer. Mark start and finish points 20 mm apart.
  3. Place a poppy seed at the oral end and time its travel (3 trials). This is the control.
  4. Add 2–3 drops of ACh, wait 5 min and take 3 readings. Wash with Ringer and allow 10 min.
  5. Repeat with physostigmine; then atropine; then ACh in the presence of atropine.
