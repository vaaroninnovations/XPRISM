MODULE 05 Examination 5: To Study the Anticonvulsant Activity of a Given Drug Against Pentylenetetrazole (PTZ)-Induced Seizures in Mice
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Anticonvulsant Screening by the Pentylenetetrazole (Chemoconvulsant) Test

Pre-treat albino mice with a test drug, challenge them 30 min later with pentylenetetrazole (PTZ) 85 mg/kg s.c. and observe each mouse in its own compartment for 30 min. Time the latency to the first myoclonic jerk, to the generalised clonic convulsion and to tonic hind-limb extension, and note mortality. Compare with a vehicle group to find the % protection.

You do every step yourself: mark, weigh and randomise 12 mice (vehicle or test drug); calculate and prepare the drug and PTZ solutions; inject i.p., wait 30 min, give PTZ s.c., place each mouse in a numbered compartment and score events with the timers; then calculate % protection and mean latency and compare the groups (Fisher's exact test, t-test). Seizure signs are shown as neutral clinical observation states; terminal procedures are carried out by trained staff off-screen, following CCSEA guidelines.

1

Principle

PTZ is a non-competitive GABA-A receptor antagonist (picrotoxin site). By blocking GABAergic inhibition it produces myoclonic and clonic seizures. The test predicts efficacy against absence and myoclonic epilepsy (drugs that raise the seizure threshold), whereas MES (Exp #3) predicts efficacy against generalised tonic-clonic seizures.

2

Signs to time (modified Racine)

1 ear / facial twitching • 2 myoclonic jerks • 3 forelimb clonus • 4 generalised clonic convulsion with loss of righting reflex • 5 tonic hind-limb extension, which may be followed by death. Cut-off 30 min (1800 s).

3

Protection

A mouse is protected if it shows no generalised clonic seizure in 30 min; animals without an event receive the cut-off latency (1800 s). Compare latencies by Student's t-test and % protection by Fisher's exact test. Diazepam, phenobarbitone, valproate, ethosuximide protect; phenytoin does not.

Requirements

  • Albino mice 20–30 g, 6 per group (vehicle, test drug)
  • Convulsant: pentylenetetrazole 8.5 mg/mL in saline (85 mg/kg = 10 mL/kg s.c.)
  • Drugs (i.p., 10 mL/kg): diazepam 4 mg/kg, ethosuximide 150 mg/kg, phenobarbitone 30 mg/kg, phenytoin 25 mg/kg, sodium valproate 200 mg/kg, or the coded Drug X
  • Two 6-compartment perspex observation boxes, stopwatch, analytical and animal balances, 1 mL syringes with 26 G needles, picric acid

Procedure

  1. Mark, weigh and randomise 12 mice into vehicle (6) and test drug (6) groups.
  2. Calculate and make up 10 mL of the drug solution and 10 mL of PTZ 8.5 mg/mL; work out each dose volume.
  3. Inject saline or drug i.p. and note the time; wait 30 min.
  4. Inject PTZ 85 mg/kg s.c. (loose skin of the neck) and place each mouse at once in its own compartment.
  5. Watch for 30 min; press the event buttons at the first jerk, clonic convulsion, tonic extension and mortality.
  6. Record the latencies (1800 s if none), then calculate % protection, mean latency, Fisher's exact test and t-test.
