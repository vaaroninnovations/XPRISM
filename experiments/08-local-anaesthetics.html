MODULE 08 Examination 08: To Compare Local Anaesthetics by Surface (Rabbit Cornea), Infiltration (Guinea-Pig Wheal) and Nerve-Block (Frog Plexus) Methods
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Evaluation of Local Anaesthetic Activity by Three Methods

Compare the onset and duration of local anaesthesia produced by lignocaine, procaine and tetracaine, and the effect of adding adrenaline, using: (A) surface anaesthesia on the rabbit cornea, (B) infiltration anaesthesia in guinea-pig skin wheals and (C) nerve-block anaesthesia of the frog's lumbar plexus.

A

Surface: rabbit cornea

Instil the drug into the conjunctival sac of one eye (other eye: saline). Touch the cornea 6 times with a cotton wisp every 5 min. Loss of blink (0/6) = anaesthesia. Procaine penetrates mucosa poorly and is ineffective topically.

B

Infiltration: guinea pig

Raise 0.2 mL intradermal wheals on the shaved back. Prick each wheal 6 times every 5 min; absence of the skin twitch (panniculus reflex) = anaesthesia. Adrenaline (vasoconstrictor) prolongs the block.

C

Nerve block: frog plexus

Inject the drug into the abdominal lymph sac near the lumbar plexus of a pithed frog. Dip each foot into 0.1 N HCl and note the withdrawal time (cut-off 10 s). A treated leg that fails to withdraw is blocked.

Drugs & animals

  • Rabbits (1.5–2 kg): lignocaine 4%, procaine 2%, tetracaine 0.5% (3 drops)
  • Guinea pig (400–600 g): lignocaine 1%, lignocaine 1% + adrenaline 1:100 000, procaine 1%, saline (0.2 mL i.d.)
  • Frogs (pithed): lignocaine 1%, procaine 2% (0.5 mL into lymph sac); 0.1 N HCl
  • Cotton wisp / bristle, 26 G needle, stop-watch

Definitions used

  • Onset: time from drug application to the first test with complete loss of response (0/6, or no withdrawal within 10 s).
  • Duration: time from onset to the first later test in which the response returns (≥ 3/6 responses, or withdrawal within 10 s).
  • Test every 5 min until the response is fully restored.
