MODULE 26 Examination 26: To Study Drug-Induced Gene Expression by Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) Analysis
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) Analysis of Drug-Induced Gene Expression

Measure how a drug changes the expression of a target gene, working from the biological sample to the final result. Select tissue or cells, separate RNA and DNA by the acid guanidinium-phenol-chloroform (TRIzol) method, quantify the RNA by UV absorbance, prepare cDNA by reverse transcription, perform real-time PCR (qPCR), and check your products by gel electrophoresis of DNA and RNA. Calculate the fold change by the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Every stage feeds the next. Poor technique early on, such as RNase contamination, carry-over of the interphase or a skipped DNase step, shows up later in your purity ratios, gels, controls and Ct values. Tissue, cells and primers are simulated: no animal is used.

1

Selection of tissue or cells

RNA degrades quickly, so pick healthy, well-preserved material. Tissue: firm, uniform liver, 20–30 mg, kept cold. Cells: a healthy monolayer at 70–90% confluence, counted with trypan blue.
Cells/mL = mean count per large square × dilution × 104

2

Separation of RNA and DNA

Chloroform and centrifugation split the acidic TRIzol lysate into three phases: an upper clear aqueous phase (RNA), a white interphase (DNA) and a lower pink organic phase (protein, DNA). RNA is precipitated with isopropanol; DNA with ethanol.

3

Quantification of RNA

1 A260 unit (1 cm path) = 40 µg/mL RNA or 50 µg/mL double-stranded DNA.
RNA (ng/µL) = A260 × 40
Pure RNA: A260/280 ≈ 2.0 (low = protein or phenol) and A260/230 ≈ 2.0–2.2 (low = guanidine or ethanol).

4

Preparation of cDNA

Reverse transcriptase copies 1 µg of RNA into cDNA in 20 µL: 25 °C 10 min (primer annealing), 42 °C 60 min (synthesis), 85 °C 5 min (enzyme inactivation).
RNA volume (µL) = 1000 ÷ conc. (ng/µL)

5

PCR analysis

SYBR Green fluorescence rises with product each cycle. Ct is the cycle at which it crosses the threshold; lower Ct means more template.
ΔCt = Cttarget − Ctreference
ΔΔCt = ΔCttreated − ΔCtcontrol
Fold change = 2−ΔΔCt

6

Gel electrophoresis

Negatively charged nucleic acids run towards the red (+) electrode; small molecules travel furthest. RNA gel: sharp 28S and 18S bands at about 2:1 show intact RNA. DNA gel: one band of the expected size confirms the PCR product.

Requirements

  • Sample set A: rat liver, vehicle or phenobarbitone 80 mg/kg/day i.p. for 4 days; target Cyp2b1; reference Gapdh
  • Sample set B: RAW 264.7 macrophages, medium or LPS 100 ng/mL for 4 h; target Tnf; reference Gapdh
  • TRIzol, chloroform, isopropanol, 75% and 100% ethanol, 0.1 M sodium citrate in 10% ethanol, 8 mM NaOH, RNase-free water, DNase I
  • Refrigerated microcentrifuge, vortex, heat block, micropipettes with filter tips, UV microvolume spectrophotometer
  • Reverse transcriptase kit, thermal cycler, 2× SYBR Green master mix, primers, real-time PCR instrument
  • Agarose, TAE and MOPS buffers, formaldehyde, nucleic acid stain, DNA and RNA ladders, gel tank, power pack, UV gel documentation system

Good laboratory practice

  1. RNases are everywhere and very stable. Wear fresh gloves, treat the bench with an RNase decontaminant, use filter tips and keep samples on ice.
  2. Phenol, chloroform and formaldehyde are toxic, and nucleic acid stains may be mutagenic: use a fume hood and protective equipment.
  3. Take only about 80–90% of the aqueous phase. Touching the interphase carries DNA and protein into your RNA.
  4. Run two controls: NTC (no template, detects contamination) and −RT (no reverse transcriptase, detects genomic DNA).
  5. Report results as mean ΔCt of three biological replicates, each measured in technical triplicate.
