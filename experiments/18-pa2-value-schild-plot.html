MODULE 18 Examination 18: To Determine the pA2 Value of Atropine Against Acetylcholine Using Rat Ileum (Schild Plot)
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

pA2 of Atropine by Schild Plot Analysis

Record the ACh log dose–response curve on isolated rat ileum. Repeat it with three increasing concentrations of atropine in the bath. From the parallel right shifts calculate dose ratios (DR), draw the Schild plot (log(DR − 1) vs log[B]) and read pA2, the negative log of the molar antagonist concentration that gives DR = 2.

1

Principle

For a competitive antagonist the Schild equation holds: DR − 1 = [B] / KB. Taking logs: log(DR − 1) = log[B] − log KB. The plot of log(DR − 1) against log[B] is a straight line with slope ≈ 1.

2

pA2

pA2 = −log of the molar antagonist concentration that needs a doubling of agonist dose (DR = 2). It is the x-intercept of the Schild plot (sign reversed). For a competitive antagonist with slope 1, pA2 = pKB. Atropine on ileum: ≈ 9.

3

Checks

Slope not different from 1 and parallel shifts with the same maximum confirm simple competitive antagonism. A slope well below 1 suggests uptake or receptor heterogeneity. A depressed maximum suggests non-competitive block.

Requirements

  • Rat ileum segment 2–3 cm, Tyrode at 37 °C, aerated
  • Organ bath 20 mL, isotonic lever (0.5 g), kymograph
  • Acetylcholine 0.1, 1, 10, 100 µg/mL
  • Atropine sulphate giving 3 × 10−9, 10−8 and 3 × 10−8 M in the bath (in the Tyrode reservoir)
  • Graph paper or spreadsheet for linear regression

Procedure

  1. Equilibrate the ileum for 30 min and record a control ACh DRC to the maximum.
  2. Change to Tyrode containing atropine 3 × 10−9 M, equilibrate 15 min and repeat the DRC.
  3. Repeat with 10−8 M and then 3 × 10−8 M atropine (increasing concentrations, no washout in between).
  4. Read EC50 for each curve and calculate DR = EC50(B) ÷ EC50(control).
  5. Plot log(DR − 1) against log[B]; fit a straight line.
  6. pA2 = −(x-intercept). Check that the slope is close to 1.
