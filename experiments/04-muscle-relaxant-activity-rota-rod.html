MODULE 04 Examination 4: To Study the Muscle Relaxant Activity of a Given Drug Using the Rota-Rod Apparatus
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Evaluation of Skeletal Muscle Relaxant Activity & Motor Coordination Using the Rota-Rod

Assess the effect of centrally acting muscle relaxants and CNS depressants—the benzodiazepine standard Diazepam (4 mg/kg i.p.), the phenothiazine Chlorpromazine (3 mg/kg i.p.) and the barbiturate Phenobarbitone sodium (40 mg/kg i.p.)—on the motor coordination (grip strength and balance) of albino mice placed on a rotating rod. Caffeine (10 mg/kg i.p.) serves as a negative comparator. You do every step yourself: catch, weigh and mark the mice, screen them on the rod, prepare the drug, calculate and inject each dose, wait for absorption, run the post-drug trial and record every reading. Mistakes in technique show up in your data.

1

Principle

A normal mouse keeps its balance on a slowly rotating knurled rod (≈3 cm diameter, 20–25 rpm) indefinitely by walking against the rotation. Drugs that relax skeletal muscle or impair motor coordination reduce grip and balance, so the animal falls off sooner. The time to fall (fall-off time) is the index of muscle relaxant activity.

2

Screening & Design

Only mice that remain on the rod for the full cut-off time (180 s) during the basal trial are accepted; failures go back to the stock cage and a spare is screened (Reduction: no animal is wasted on unusable data). Each mouse acts as its own control: the basal fall-off time is recorded, the drug is injected i.p., and the post-drug fall-off time is recorded 30 min later at the same speed (n = 6).

3

Calculation

Dose volume (mL) = body wt (kg) × dose (mg/kg) ÷ concentration (mg/mL). % Decrease in motor coordination = ((Basal − Post-drug) / Basal) × 100. Report Mean ± SEM and compare basal vs post-drug values by Student's paired t-test (df = n − 1; tcrit = 2.571 for df 5).

Requirements

  • Apparatus: Rota-rod (6 compartments, knurled rod 3 cm, variable 5–40 rpm, trip-plate timers, settable cut-off)
  • Animals: Albino mice, 20–28 g, either sex (n = 6 accepted after screening; spares in the stock cage)
  • Drugs (you prepare 10 mL): Diazepam 1 mg/mL (suspension: 1 drop Tween 80 + normal saline), Chlorpromazine HCl 0.5 mg/mL, Phenobarbitone sodium 4 mg/mL, Caffeine citrate 1 mg/mL (all in normal saline)
  • Others: top-pan balance with weighing beaker, analytical balance, mortar & pestle, 10 mL volumetric flask, 1 mL tuberculin syringe with 26G needle, saturated picric acid, wall clock

Procedure

  1. Weigh each mouse in a tared beaker and number it with picric acid (head = 1, neck = 2, back = 3 …).
  2. Switch ON the rota-rod and set the speed to 20–25 rpm and the cut-off to 180 s.
  3. Place one mouse in each compartment, press START and note the basal fall-off time. Accept mice that stay 180 s; replace failures.
  4. Prepare the drug, calculate each dose volume from body weight and inject i.p. (lower right quadrant, 26G needle, 30–45°). Note the time.
  5. After 30 min, repeat the trial at the same speed and cut-off.
  6. Tabulate, calculate % decrease and mean ± SEM, apply the paired t-test.
Ethics (CCSEA, 3Rs): handle mice by the base of the tail and support the body; never exceed the cut-off (prolonged forced walking causes fatigue and stress); keep i.p. volumes ≤ 10 mL/kg. This virtual practical replaces animal use for training.
