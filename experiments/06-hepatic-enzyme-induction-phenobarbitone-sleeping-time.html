MODULE 06 Examination 06: To Study the Effect of an Enzyme Inducer (Phenobarbitone) and an Inhibitor (Cimetidine) on Pentobarbitone Sleeping Time in Mice
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Effect of Microsomal Enzyme Inducers and Inhibitors on Barbiturate Sleeping Time

Pretreat groups of mice with saline, phenobarbitone (an inducer of hepatic CYP enzymes, 3 days) or cimetidine (a CYP inhibitor, 1 h before). Then give a standard hypnotic dose of pentobarbitone sodium (40 mg/kg i.p.) and measure the onset (loss of righting reflex) and sleeping time (loss to recovery of righting reflex).

1

Principle

Pentobarbitone action is terminated mainly by hepatic oxidation (CYP450). Its sleeping time is therefore an index of hepatic drug-metabolising activity.

2

Inducer

Phenobarbitone (repeated dosing) increases synthesis of CYP2B/3A enzymes and smooth ER. Pentobarbitone is metabolised faster, so sleeping time is shortened. Induction takes days to develop.

3

Inhibitor

Cimetidine binds the haem iron of CYP enzymes and inhibits oxidation. Pentobarbitone persists longer, so sleeping time is prolonged. Inhibition is rapid (within hours).

Requirements

  • Swiss albino mice 20–30 g, either sex, 6 per group
  • Phenobarbitone sodium 80 mg/kg/day i.p. × 3 days (8 mg/mL)
  • Cimetidine 100 mg/kg i.p. 1 h before (10 mg/mL)
  • Pentobarbitone sodium 40 mg/kg i.p. (4 mg/mL, 0.1 mL/10 g)
  • Normal saline, 1 mL syringes, balance, stop-watch, observation cages

Procedure

  1. Divide 18 mice into 3 groups. Give saline (Gr A) or phenobarbitone (Gr B) daily for 3 days.
  2. On day 4, give cimetidine to Gr C and wait 60 min.
  3. Weigh each mouse and inject pentobarbitone 40 mg/kg i.p. Note the time.
  4. Place the mouse on its back every 1–2 min. Loss of righting reflex (stays on its back > 30 s) = onset of sleep.
  5. Note when the mouse rights itself spontaneously = recovery.
  6. Sleeping time = recovery − onset. Compare groups (mean ± SEM, unpaired t-test).
