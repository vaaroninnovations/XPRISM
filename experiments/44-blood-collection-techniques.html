Blood Collection Techniques in Laboratory Animals

Four sample requests are pinned above the bench: a blood count and serum enzymes from Wistar rats, and a glucose and a clotting sample from Swiss albino mice. For each animal, weigh it, work out the largest safe volume, choose a humane site, collect the blood with the right instrument, stop the bleeding, put the blood in the right tube and prepare it for the laboratory. A good sample is useless if the animal was harmed, and a humane sample is useless if it is haemolysed or in the wrong tube.

1

Safe volume

Circulating blood volume ≈ 64 mL/kg (rat) or 72 mL/kg (mouse). A single sample should not exceed 10% of it; the animal then needs about two weeks to recover. Repeated weekly samples: up to 7.5%.

2

Site

Conscious survival sampling uses superficial veins: the lateral tail vein (warm the tail first), the saphenous vein, and in mice the submandibular vein. Retro-orbital bleeding needs anaesthesia and is discouraged; cardiac puncture is terminal only.

3

Tube

The test decides the tube. Serum needs blood with no anticoagulant to clot for about 30 minutes before spinning. Tubes with anticoagulant are inverted gently 8–10 times. Citrate must be filled exactly to its line (9 : 1).

4

Quality and refinement

Avoid haemolysis: gentle suction, take the needle off before filling the tube, never shake. Press with gauze until bleeding stops. Spin at about 1000–2000 × g for 10–15 minutes.
