MODULE 01 Examination 1: To Study the Effect of Different Routes of Drug Administration in Mice
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Effect of Route of Administration on the Onset and Duration of Drug Action

Administer the same dose of pentobarbitone sodium (40 mg/kg) to albino mice by five routes—intravenous, intraperitoneal, intramuscular, subcutaneous and oral—and record the onset of loss of righting reflex (onset of sleep) and the duration of sleep (time until the righting reflex returns).

1

Principle

The route decides how fast and how much drug reaches the circulation. The i.v. route bypasses absorption (100% bioavailability, immediate onset). Parenteral routes depend on blood flow at the site (i.p. > i.m. > s.c.). The oral route is slowest and is reduced by GI absorption and hepatic first-pass metabolism.

2

Righting Reflex

A normal mouse placed on its back rights itself at once. Loss of righting reflex (LRR), failing to right within 30 s, marks the onset of hypnosis. Its return marks the end of sleeping time.

3

Design

Five groups (n = 3 per route). Weigh each mouse, calculate the dose volume from the stock strength, inject by the correct technique, then shift the mouse to its observation cage. Report Mean ± SEM onset and duration for each route.
