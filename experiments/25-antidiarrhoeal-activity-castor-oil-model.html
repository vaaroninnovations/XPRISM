MODULE 25 Examination 25: To Evaluate the Antidiarrhoeal Activity of Loperamide and Atropine against Castor Oil-Induced Diarrhoea in Mice
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Antidiarrhoeal Activity: Castor Oil-Induced Diarrhoea

Pretreat fasted mice with vehicle, loperamide (peripheral μ-opioid agonist) or atropine (antimuscarinic). One hour later give castor oil 0.5 mL orally and place each mouse in a cage lined with blotting paper. Over 4 h record the onset of diarrhoea, the number of wet (diarrhoeal) faeces and the total number of faeces.

1

Principle

Lipases release ricinoleic acid from castor oil in the small intestine. It activates EP3 prostanoid receptors on smooth muscle, stimulates peristalsis and secretion, and causes watery diarrhoea.

2

Scoring

Wet faeces: soft, unformed droppings leaving a wet stain on the blotting paper. Formed faeces: firm pellets without a stain.
% inhibition = (Mc − Mt) ÷ Mc × 100 (M = mean wet faeces)

3

Expected

Loperamide markedly delays onset and reduces wet faeces (reduced motility, increased absorption). Atropine gives a smaller protection.

Requirements

  • Mice 20–30 g, 6 per group, fasted 18 h (water ad lib)
  • Castor oil (0.5 mL/mouse p.o.)
  • Loperamide 3 mg/kg p.o.; atropine 5 mg/kg i.p.; vehicle (saline)
  • Individual cages lined with white blotting paper, oral feeding needle

Procedure

  1. Give vehicle / drug. After 1 h give castor oil 0.5 mL orally.
  2. Place each mouse in its own cage on blotting paper.
  3. Watch for 4 h. Note the time of the first wet dropping (onset).
  4. Classify every dropping as wet or formed (the blotting paper is changed every hour).
  5. Calculate mean wet and total faeces, % inhibition; compare with control.
