MODULE 33 Examination 33: To Study Drug Effects on Conditioned Avoidance Using Cook's Pole Climbing Apparatus
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Pole Climbing Test of the Conditioned Avoidance Response

A trained rat learns that a buzzer warns of a shock through the grid floor, and climbs a pole during the buzzer to avoid it. This is the conditioned avoidance response (CAR). A rat that climbs only after the shock begins shows an escape response. Antipsychotics block avoidance at doses that spare escape. Sedatives and motor-impairing doses tend to block both, so the test separates the two actions.

You do every step yourself: mark, weigh and randomise 18 trained rats; confirm that each still meets the avoidance criterion; prepare chlorpromazine and the test drug; dose each rat; run a 5-trial session 60 minutes later, pressing the key when the rat reaches the pole; and analyse by one-way ANOVA and Dunnett's test.

1

Principle

The method follows Cook and Weidley (1957). The buzzer is the conditioned stimulus (CS) and the shock is the unconditioned stimulus (UCS). Selective loss of avoidance with escape intact is the classic screening profile of an antipsychotic. It is linked to dopamine D2 blockade in the mesolimbic system.

2

Measures

CAR: climbs during the 10 s buzzer. Escape: climbs during the shock. No response: stays on the grid until the 30 s cut-off. Latency: time from buzzer onset to the pole.

% CAR = CAR trials ÷ 5 × 100

3

Design and statistics

I: vehicle. II: standard, chlorpromazine 10 mg/kg. III: test drug. Use one-way ANOVA followed by Dunnett's test against Group I. Scores out of 5 are counts, so a research report would normally add a non-parametric test such as Kruskal-Wallis.

Requirements

  • Trained Wistar albino rats 180–250 g, 6 per group (18 in all)
  • Normal saline, chlorpromazine 10 mg/kg
  • Test drugs: haloperidol 0.5 mg/kg, phenobarbitone 60 mg/kg, diazepam 5 mg/kg, imipramine 15 mg/kg (all i.p., 2 mL/kg)
  • Cook's pole climbing apparatus: grid-floor chamber, central pole, buzzer and shock source; 70% ethanol, balances, 1 mL syringes

Procedure

  1. Mark, weigh and randomise. Set the grid to the lowest shock that drives reliable climbing.
  2. Run a 5-trial baseline. A rat needs at least 4 CAR; retrain any that falls short.
  3. Prepare the solutions and inject i.p. about 8 minutes apart, because each rat is tested 60 minutes after its own injection.
  4. Place the rat on the grid and press START TEST. Press RAT ON POLE as soon as all four paws leave the grid.
  5. Clean the chamber with 70% ethanol between rats, then analyse CAR and escape together.
