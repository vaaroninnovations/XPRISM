MODULE 03 Examination 3: Anticonvulsant Screening by Maximal Electroshock Seizure (MES)
EXPERIMENT PROTOCOL & CURRICULUM

Evaluation of Anticonvulsant Activity of Drugs Using Electroconvulsometer (MES Model)

The Maximal Electroshock Seizure (MES) method is the gold standard preclinical screening model for testing anti-epileptic drugs primarily effective against generalized tonic-clonic (grand mal) seizures and partial convulsions. Abolition or significant abbreviation of the Hindlimb Tonic Extensor (HTE) phase serves as the clinical hallmark of anticonvulsant efficacy.

1

Electrical Induction

Standard current of 30 mA is administered transauricularly via ear-clip electrodes moistened with 0.9% saline for a duration of 0.2 seconds to induce maximal generalized seizures in Swiss albino mice.

2

Seizure Chronology

Carefully record the durations of: (a) Tonic Flexion, (b) Tonic Hindlimb Extension (usually 10–14 s in controls), (c) Clonic Convulsions (rhythmic limb clonus), and (d) Postictal Stupor.

3

Protective Index

Antiepileptic drugs such as Phenytoin (voltage-gated $Na^+$ channel blocker) selectively prevent the spread of seizure discharge, abolishing tonic hindlimb extension ($0.0 \text{ s}$) while preserving mild clonus.

Preclinical Standard Parameters (Swiss Albino Mice)

Shock Current: 30 mA AC
Shock Duration: 0.2 seconds
Electrodes: Ear-clip / Corneal with Saline
Animal Weight: 20–30 g (Fasted)

Pathophysiological Phases of Maximal Electroshock Seizure (MES)

  1. Tonic Flexor Phase: Sudden tonic contraction with forward flexion of both forelimbs and hindlimbs (~1.5–3.0 s).
  2. Tonic Extensor Phase: Rapid, rigid hyperextension of both hindlimbs pointing posteriorly 180° parallel to the spine (~10.0–14.0 s). Drug protection is marked when extension is abolished or reduced to <2 s.
  3. Clonic Jerks: Generalized intermittent, rapid, violent tremors and rhythmic paddle-like clonic jerking (~12.0–18.0 s).
  4. Postictal Stupor: Animal falls prostrate, motionless, exhausted with diminished reflexes before regaining righting reflex.
