MODULE 03 Examination 3: Anticonvulsant Screening by Maximal Electroshock Seizure (MES)
EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Evaluation of Anticonvulsant Activity of Drugs Using the Electroconvulsometer (Maximal Electroshock Seizure, MES)

The maximal electroshock seizure (MES) test is the standard preclinical screen for drugs against generalised tonic-clonic (grand mal) and partial seizures. A supramaximal electrical stimulus is applied through ear-clip or corneal electrodes; abolition of the tonic hind-limb extension (HTE) is the end-point that shows protection.

You do every step yourself: take mice from the stock cage, mark them with picric acid, weigh them and allocate them by drawing lots (vehicle or test drug); calculate and prepare the drug solution; inject each mouse i.p.; wait 30 min; then set the electroconvulsometer (50 mA, 0.2 s), wet the electrodes with saline, apply the stimulus and time the phases of the seizure with the chronometer: tonic flexion, tonic hind-limb extension, clonus and recovery. Finally enter your timings, calculate % protection and compare the groups by Fisher's exact test and the t-test. Seizure phases are shown as neutral clinical observation states; terminal procedures are carried out by trained staff off-screen, following CCSEA guidelines.

1

Electrical induction

A 50 Hz alternating current (50 mA for 0.2 s as set in this practical) is passed through ear-clip electrodes wetted with 0.9% saline (corneal electrodes need a drop of local anaesthetic first). Dry electrodes have a very high impedance and no current flows; too low a current gives only a sub-maximal seizure.

2

Phases to time

Tonic flexion (~1.5–3 s) → tonic hind-limb extension (~10–14 s in controls; hind limbs rigid, parallel to the tail) → clonus (~12–18 s) → post-ictal depression until the righting reflex returns (~1–1.5 min). Protected mice skip the extension phase.

3

Expected

Phenytoin (Na⁺-channel block) abolishes HTE and spares clonus. Valproate abolishes HTE and shortens clonus. Diazepam (GABA-A) abolishes HTE, suppresses clonus and prolongs the sedated post-ictal period.

Requirements

  • Swiss albino mice 20–30 g, 6 per group (vehicle, test drug)
  • Vehicle: 0.9% saline 10 mL/kg i.p.
  • Test drugs (i.p., 10 mL/kg): phenytoin sodium 25 mg/kg, sodium valproate 300 mg/kg, diazepam 4 mg/kg, or the coded Drug X
  • Electroconvulsometer with ear-clip / corneal electrodes, 0.9% saline, saline gauze, proparacaine 0.5% (corneal)
  • Analytical balance, animal balance, 1 mL syringes + 26 G needles, picric acid, chronometer

Procedure

  1. Mark, weigh and randomise 12 mice into vehicle (6) and test drug (6) groups.
  2. Calculate and make up 10 mL of the drug solution (mg/mL = dose ÷ 10 mL/kg); work out each dose volume (10 mL/kg × weight).
  3. Inject saline or drug i.p. and note the time; wait 30 min.
  4. Switch on the stimulator, set 50 mA and 0.2 s, wet the electrodes with saline, attach them to the mouse and press SHOCK.
  5. Press the phase buttons as each phase begins (flexion, extension, clonus, end of clonus, righting reflex).
  6. Calculate each duration, record whether HTE occurred, then work out % protection, mean extension, Fisher's exact test and t-test.
